Host Comfort Survival Kit

You can price every mug to the nickel and still limp into the house at 3 p.m. because you spent six hours standing on concrete in yesterday's sneakers, with no shade and a Diet Coke you forgot to drink. Shoppers get to leave. You do not.

This is a timed comfort plan for the host, from the night before through teardown. Pair it with the Weather Plan if the forecast is being dramatic, and check your city guide for hours, because the official start time is when your feet need to be ready, not when you planned to 'ease into it.'

The night before: set the station, not just the tables

Build a host station within two steps of the cash box: water, a snack that will not melt into a smear, sunscreen, a hat, a spare marker, and a phone charger or power bank. If you will stand in one spot, lay an anti-fatigue mat there now, while you still have patience.

Lay out tomorrow's clothes like you are leaving for a flight at dawn. Sun shirt, broken-in shoes, socks you trust. New shoes are a joke you will not find funny at 11 a.m.

6:00 a.m.: you are already on the clock

Eat something with protein. Fill two bottles and put one in a cooler with ice, not on a sunny table. If the sale is in full sun, the canopy goes up now, with weights on every leg, before the first early bird tries to 'just peek' while you are holding a pole.

Do a five-minute stretch you would laugh at in a gym and thank yourself for later: calves, lower back, shoulders. You are about to reach, lift, and stand.

Opening to noon: manage heat, not heroics

Set a timer to drink water every 30 minutes. Thirst is a late signal. If you are running the cash, sit on a high stool for stretches of ten minutes. Sitting is allowed. You are not a statue.

A neck fan sounds silly until the driveway starts shimmering. Shade plus moving air plus water will keep you civil when a shopper wants to haggle over fifty cents for the fourth time.

Rotate jobs with a helper if you have one: cashier, floater, restock. The floater is the person who can actually go to the bathroom. Put that on a schedule, because 'I'll go in a minute' becomes 2 p.m.

Afternoon: the crash window

The crowd thins, the sun does not, and this is when hosts get sloppy: they skip lunch, sit on a low curb, and then cannot stand back up without a sound they did not intend to make. Eat. Reapply sunscreen. If the forecast picked up, get the tarp ready before the first drop, not during it.

If you feel dizzy, sloppy, or angry at harmless questions, you are overheated or underfed. Hand the cash box to someone else and sit in actual shade for ten minutes. The $8 lamp will still be there.

Teardown is part of the workday

Leave 45 minutes of body in the tank for carrying tables back in. Drink the last bottle. Do not 'just finish' in bare feet. Tomorrow's stiffness is often the leftover of today's stubbornness.

When the last table is in, sit down before you count the money. Counting while you wobble is how a twenty walks off the pile.
