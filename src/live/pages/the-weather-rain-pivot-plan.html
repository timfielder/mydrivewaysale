Weather Plan

The forecast said 'a 20 percent chance,' which is meteorologist for 'we would like to keep our options open.' Your tables are already in the driveway, the first sign is at the corner, and a dark line of cloud is doing laps at the edge of the subdivision.

A weather plan is a set of if/then calls you make before you are holding a dripping cardboard box. Check your city guide for official hours, because some towns will not let you start at dawn just to beat a storm, and some will not let you stay late to make up for a washout.

Twelve hours out

If the overnight forecast puts steady rain over your opening hour, you have three honest options: move the date, shrink the sale to a garage or a covered porch, or open later if your local rules allow it. Post the change on the same listings you already used. People would rather know at 8 p.m. than stand in your puddle at 8 a.m.

If the forecast is iffy, stage the sale as if it will rain for twenty minutes at the worst possible time. That means:

  • A clear tarp folded at the top of the driveway, not buried in the garage under bikes.
  • Weights for every canopy leg. An empty canopy in a gust is a sail, and it will take a table with it.
  • Electronics, paper, and anything that swells when wet staged on a cart you can roll inside in one trip.
  • A 'rain table' plan: which items can sit it out, and which get pulled first.

Two hours out

Look at the sky and a radar app, then pick a lane. If a narrow band is going to pass at 9:15, delay the curb signs, keep the weather-sensitive items inside, and tell early birds you open after the shower. If the morning is clearly washed out and the afternoon is clean, shift your energy to a later start rather than heroically soggy merchandising.

Wind is the quieter problem. If flags are snapping and the forecast mentions gusts, skip a tall canopy or take it down. Tie lightweight items so they do not become projectiles. Clothes on hangers need clips. Stacked plastic bins need a weight on the lid.

When the rain starts mid-sale

People will try to 'wait it out' under your canopy, which is fine until the canopy starts to pond. Assign jobs in this order: cover the cash and the electronics, pull paper goods, drop the clear tarp over remaining tables, then worry about the folding chairs. Do not plug anything back in until it is dry.

If thunder is close enough to count, the sale pauses and people go to their cars. You can like money and still not hold a metal garment rack in an open driveway during a lightning show.

Heat, and the opposite of rain

A still, hot day will melt crayons, warp vinyl, and cook the cashier. Shade the checkout first, then the clothes, then everything else. Keep a cooler of water for the hosts. For the human side of heat, use the Host Comfort Survival Kit.

The call to pack it in

Set your walk-away rule before Saturday: if standing water is on the tables, if the wind will not let a sign stay upright, or if you cannot keep the cash dry, you are done. Take the curb signs down so stragglers are not arriving to a half-collapsed sale. You can always run a rain-check hour later in the day if the rules and the sky both allow it.
