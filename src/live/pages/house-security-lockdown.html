House Security Lockdown

A driveway sale is a standing invitation for people you do not know to come within ten feet of your front door, while a box of cash sits on a folding table and you turn your back to make change. Most weekends are boring, which is how people get casual.

Think in three layers: the house, the cash, and the close. This is practical habit, not a lecture on crime statistics. If your city has rules on signs, hours, or where you may stand with a table, check the city guide so you are not improvising after dark.

Before: lock the house you are not selling

The night before, walk every door and window you will not be using, including the side gate, the back slider, and the door from the garage into the kitchen. During the sale, that kitchen door is the one a distracted helper will leave on the latch 'so we can grab more bags.'

Clear the sale area of things you are not selling that still look expensive: packages on the porch, a laptop on the windowsill, keys on a hook you can see from the driveway, a garage wall of tools that are off limits. If you do not want to answer 'how much for the bike on the hook,' take the bike off the hook.

Set a bathroom plan that does not send shoppers through the house. A Porta-John is rare at a home sale. A locked house and a helper who can pause the table for five minutes is the usual solution.

During: two sets of eyes, one cash rule

If you can, run the sale with two people. One stays on the box. The other floats, watches the far table, and notices the shopper who is more interested in the open garage than in the lamp. If you are solo, set the tables so you can see the house door and the cash at the same time, even if the layout is a little uglier.

Cash stays in a locking box, not a shoebox, and the box stays in reach of the cashier, not 'just inside the garage.' When the bills start to pile up, skim the large ones into the house on a schedule, like once an hour, and do it without an announcement. Leave enough change to keep the line moving.

Mark a bill, or at least look at it, before it goes in the box. Quick-change attempts rely on speed and your manners. It is fine to say, 'hang on, I want to recount that with you.' A counterfeit-looking bill gets a polite no and a request for another form of payment.

Do not leave the box to help someone load a dresser. Either the dresser waits, or the helper does the loading. The person who wants you away from the cash will be very patient about the dresser.

After: close the invitation

Take the curb signs down when you close, even if leftover items are still in the driveway. A sign at the corner after dusk is how you get late knocks you do not want. Move remaining items inside or out of sight the same day.

Count the cash indoors, not at the curb. Wait until the last shopper has actually left the block. Then lock the doors you already locked this morning, because teardown is when they get left open.

If you also want the walking-surface hazards handled, use the Driveway Liability Checklist. A safe sale is a locked door and a driveway people can walk without going down.
