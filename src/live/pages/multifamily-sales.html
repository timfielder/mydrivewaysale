Multi-Family Sales

A multi-family sale can empty three attics in one morning, or it can end with two households arguing over a $4 blender. The difference is almost never the quality of the stuff. It is whether you decided, in writing, who owns which table, which color, and which pile of cash.

Treat this like a tiny joint venture that lasts 48 hours. Check your city guide before you pick a date, because some towns count a shared sale against each household's yearly limit, and some want a permit in one name.

Pick a lead address and a lead human

One driveway, one official contact, one person who can answer a shopper asking 'is the dresser still available?' If you rotate yards, put that in the listing so people are not standing on the wrong porch. The lead human also holds the permit, if your city wants one, and keeps a copy of everyone's phone numbers.

Color is how you stay friends at 4 p.m.

Give each household a sticker color, and only that color. Household A is green, B is blue, C is yellow. Price tags sit on the item. When a shopper walks up with a mixed armload, you are adding colors, not memories of who brought the lamp.

Put a legend on a poster at the checkout: green is the Ramirez family, blue is the Cole family, and so on. Helpers should be able to run the table without asking, 'wait, whose was the red sticker?'

If you want the math done by a tap instead of a scratchpad, the free StickerCalc Lite tool lets you assign a dollar value and a label to each color, then tap the colors as people buy. It will not settle a family argument for you. It will keep the totals honest.

Cash, change, and the ugly envelopes

Start with a shared change bank, counted together, and write the starting amount on a card in the cash box. Every household chips in the same amount. At the end, pull that starter cash back out first, then split the rest by color totals.

Do not dump all cash into one jar and 'figure it out later.' Later is when everyone is tired and the $20s look the same. A simple tally sheet, or the session total in StickerCalc if you used color values correctly, is the adult move.

If one household is selling a $200 mower and another is selling paperbacks, color totals already handle the imbalance. You do not need a spreadsheet. You need the stickers to stay on until the item leaves the driveway.

Parking, bathrooms, and the neighbor truce

Tell the block what you are doing, especially if three households of shoppers are about to arrive at one corner. Agree in advance on overflow parking, who restocks toilet paper, and who shuts it down if the crowd spills into a no-parking area. If an HOA or a city rule limits signs or hours, the lead human is the one who has actually read it.

Close like you opened: together

Set a hard end time. Count the box together. Photograph the tally. Return the starter cash. Send each household home with their unsold items the same day, because 'I'll grab it tomorrow' is how a garage stays full. Then text the group one number: what each color earned, after the change bank.
